## Gradients

Two restrained gradients are exposed as `bg-brand-bg` and `bg-brand-accent`.

- Use the background gradient for occasional page headers, onboarding, or large empty areas.
- Use the accent gradient for a small premium highlight or visualization accent.
- Never use a gradient on routine POS buttons, tables, financial totals, form fields, or body-copy backgrounds.
- Use at most one gradient on a screen.

## Tone and voice

Use direct, specific language and active verbs. Keep one idea per sentence. Prefer “3 transfers need approval” over vague calls to action.

Sound confident and considered, never luxurious or ornamental. Avoid hype words, urgency, filler, exclamation marks, and claims such as “seamless” or “powerful.”

Example: “Three transfers are waiting for approval. Review them when you’re ready.”

## Usage rules

**Do:**

- Use semantic tokens rather than hardcoded colors.
- Use a consistent four-pixel spacing grid.
- Keep cards flat with quiet borders and generous internal spacing.
- Use `font-mono tabular-nums` for changing values and identifiers.
- Test light and dark modes at mobile, tablet, and desktop widths.

**Don’t:**

- Mix warm and cool neutral systems.
- Add extra brand colors when a semantic status color is intended.
- Animate gradients or use `transition-all`.
- Put body text on the accent gradient.
- Use decorative serif typography in operational screens.
